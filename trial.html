Convex & Concave Lenses

Lenses are transparent objects that can change the direction of light by refraction. They are used in many optical devices, including cameras, glasses and microscopes.

Types of Lenses

There are two main types of lenses:

  • Convex lenses — thicker in the middle than at the edges.
  • Concave lenses — thinner in the middle than at the edges.

Definition

Refraction is the change in direction of a wave when it passes from one medium into another.

Convex Lenses

A convex lens is thicker in the middle and thinner at the edges. It causes parallel rays of light to move towards each other.

This means that a convex lens is a converging lens.

Key Idea

A convex lens causes parallel rays of light to converge towards a point called the principal focus.

Concave Lenses

A concave lens is thinner in the middle and thicker at the edges. It causes parallel rays of light to spread out.

This means that a concave lens is a diverging lens.

Comparing the Two Lenses

  • Convex lenses are converging lenses.
  • Concave lenses are diverging lenses.
  • Convex lenses are thicker in the middle.
  • Concave lenses are thinner in the middle.

Principal Focus

The principal focus is the point where rays of light converge after passing through a convex lens, or the point from which rays appear to diverge after passing through a concave lens.

\[ f = \frac{1}{P} \]

Where:

f = focal length in metres (m)

P = power of the lens in dioptres (D)

Worked Example

Example

A lens has a power of \(2.0\,\text{D}\). Calculate its focal length.

Start with:

\[ f = \frac{1}{P} \]

Substitute the value of the power:

\[ f = \frac{1}{2.0} \]

Therefore:

\[ f = 0.50\,\text{m} \]

The focal length of the lens is 0.50 m.

Ray Diagrams

Ray diagrams are used to determine the position and characteristics of an image formed by a lens.

  1. Draw the principal axis.
  2. Draw the lens in the correct position.
  3. Mark the principal focus.
  4. Draw the appropriate rays.
  5. Use the intersection of the rays to determine the image.

Examiner Tip

Always draw the principal axis first and label the focal points clearly. Keep your rays straight and use a ruler where possible.

Important

Make sure you distinguish between converging and diverging lenses. Mixing these up can affect the entire ray diagram.

Common Applications

  • Convex lenses are used in cameras.
  • Convex lenses are used in magnifying glasses.
  • Concave lenses can be used to correct short-sightedness.
  • Different combinations of lenses are used in microscopes and telescopes.

Practice Question

A convex lens has a focal length of \(0.25\,\text{m}\). Calculate its power.

\[ P = \frac{1}{f} \]

Answer:

\[ P = 4.0\,\text{D} \]

Summary

  • A convex lens is a converging lens.
  • A concave lens is a diverging lens.
  • The focal length is the distance from the lens to the principal focus.
  • Lens power is measured in dioptres.
